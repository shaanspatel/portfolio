Overview

This project is a 6-DOF robotic arm design study focused on the early mechanical decisions that define the rest of the system: joint layout, link geometry, drive packaging, and manufacturability. Current portfolio evidence includes a degree-of-freedom CAD view, a calculation sketch, and a custom cycloidal disk model.

Problem / Motivation

A robotic arm is a combined mechanical design and controls problem, but early choices about joint layout, actuator placement, stiffness, and transmission strategy shape everything after. This page frames the work as an engineering design study to choose an architecture that can be analyzed before committing to fabrication.

Engineering Constraints

Key constraints are reach, joint torque, stiffness, packaging, manufacturability, and serviceability. Because exact payload and performance values are not documented yet, those values should be added as design targets rather than claimed as completed specifications.

Design Approach

The approach starts with a 6-DOF layout, then uses dimensions and joint relationships to reason about range of motion and torque paths. The custom cycloidal-drive exploration indicates a focus on compact reduction and backlash control while keeping the project in an exploratory, prototype-planning stage.

CAD / Mechanical Design

The CAD images show joint axes, a base/shoulder/elbow style arrangement, and individual drive geometry being developed. The cycloidal disk asset is useful evidence of mechanism-level design work, separate from the larger arm layout.

Prototype / Fabrication

Current repo evidence supports CAD and mechanism exploration more than a finished physical build. This section should stay honest by describing the prototype state as calculation/CAD validation until fabricated links, joints, or drive tests are added.

Testing / Results

Testing results should be limited to design review and calculation checks for now: verifying link dimensions, joint clearances, approximate torque needs, and whether the drive concept is worth prototyping. Add measured motor current, backlash, payload, repeatability, or deflection only after those tests exist.

Failures / Iterations

The major iteration risk is discovering that a joint package that looks feasible in CAD becomes too bulky, too flexible, or too difficult to manufacture. The cycloidal-drive path also needs validation for tolerance sensitivity, assembly complexity, and whether the reduction strategy is worth the fabrication effort.

Lessons Learned

The arm study is useful because it shows disciplined concept development before hardware commitment. Separating arm-level architecture from drive-level experiments makes it easier to evaluate tradeoffs without overclaiming a finished robot.
