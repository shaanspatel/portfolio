Overview

This is a personal finger module concept, framed as an independent mechanical design study rather than company work. The goal is to document how a tendon-driven robotic finger could be organized as a repeatable module, while staying honest about the current state: the concept is early, in progress, and not validated.

My role / current status

My role is to define the mechanical architecture, identify the open packaging questions, and turn the idea into project documentation that can guide a future prototype. The sketches on this page are concept visuals for hand and finger mechanism discussion; they are not being presented as proof that this independent concept has been fabricated, tested, or validated.

Project development

The development work is organized around the mechanical decisions that would make one finger module understandable, buildable, and serviceable before scaling it into a hand.

  • Define a repeatable link and joint layout
  • Reserve space for tendon paths and return routing
  • Check bearing, pin, and fastener access in the stackup
  • Plan how one module would attach to a palm structure
  • Separate concept assumptions from future prototype evidence
  • Document next tests before making performance claims

Mechanism Questions

The main engineering question is how to route tendon lines through a compact articulated finger without making assembly or adjustment too fragile. The concept needs enough room for joints, tendon exits, fasteners, and service access while preserving a clear load path from the tendon to the phalange links.

CAD and packaging

The next CAD pass should focus on the smallest useful subsystem: one finger module with link geometry, joint hardware, tendon entry and exit points, and an interface to a palm or mounting plate. Keeping the model modular should make it easier to revise tendon paths or link proportions without redesigning the whole hand.

Prototype Plan

A responsible prototype step would be a single finger test module, not a full hand. That module should be used to inspect assembly access, tendon friction, joint motion, and whether the chosen hardware stackup is practical. Until that test exists, the page treats the work as concept development rather than a proven mechanism.
