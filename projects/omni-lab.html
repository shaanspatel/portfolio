Overview

This OH-MIND Lab project connects simulation, physical prototype setup, and validation imagery for a robotics research workflow. The case study focuses on what was modeled, how the robot workspace was configured, and how validation evidence was collected.

Problem / Motivation

Robotics research needs a bridge between simulated assumptions and the constraints of a real robot testbed. This project focuses on making that bridge visible through simulation scenes, lab setup, prototype/target objects, and validation snapshots that can support a research narrative without overstating final findings.

Engineering Constraints

The work is constrained by research assumptions, calibration or registration quality, sensing and lighting conditions, robot workspace limits, and the need for repeatable validation evidence. The public writeup stays qualitative until accuracy, success-rate, or timing results are cleared through approved lab records.

Design Approach

The approach is to use simulation to define or preview robot-environment interaction, then align the physical setup so the lab capture can test the same ideas. The prototype visuals suggest an iterative loop between virtual scenes, workspace setup, and validation snapshots.

CAD / Mechanical Design

The mechanical content here is the experimental environment: robot workcell, object or fixture placement, markers/target board, lighting, and the physical setup needed for repeatable data collection. The page avoids assigning fixture or prototype design ownership beyond what is documented.

Prototype / Fabrication

Prototype evidence includes a lab work surface, robot arm setup, a target/object on the table, and validation imagery. Fabrication-specific claims are limited until exact fixtures, printed parts, or mounting hardware are documented.

Testing / Results

Visible results support the existence of simulation and validation captures, but not the numerical performance of the method. A stronger public results section would include the validation protocol, sample counts, accuracy/error metrics, and research-specific results after lab approval.

Failures / Iterations

Likely iteration areas include aligning simulated assumptions to real camera/robot geometry, controlling lighting and workspace conditions, and making validation captures repeatable. Specific failure modes are described publicly only when they can be supported by approved lab notes.

Lessons Learned

The project is a good research example because it connects modeling, physical setup, and evidence collection instead of presenting simulation alone. It also shows the importance of documenting assumptions so a reviewer or recruiter can understand what was validated and what remains experimental.

Next Steps

The next useful page update is a short methods/results subsection that names the setup, protocol, and metrics after those details are cleared for public use. Until then, the page stays focused on simulation, setup, validation imagery, and research process.
